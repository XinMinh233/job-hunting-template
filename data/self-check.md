# SELF-CHECK — your weekly review of yourself

Owner: you. Claude asks the questions; you answer them and make the
change. Read by /review (weekly) and consulted by /daily when a
pattern repeats.

This file exists because the hunt this template came from measured, on
a real tracker, what delay actually costs — and the answer was
specific enough to build a habit around: leads died in the queue not
because applying was hard, but because cheap actions (an email, a page
load, a one-line question) sat unsent for days.

## The three questions (answer every week, honestly, one line each)

**1. What cheap thing did I let wait this week?**
Cheap = an email, a page load, a one-line question. Not a work
session. If the answer is "nothing", check the tracker before
believing yourself.

**2. What did I do that FELT like progress but shipped nothing?**
Logging a JD. Scoring a JD. Polishing a tool. Reading about a gap. All
of these feel productive and none of them are an application, an
artifact, or an answer.

**3. Did my time-to-apply go up or down?**
Median days from logging a role to applying. It is the one number that
measures the real constraint. Up = stop scouting, stop building,
convert.

## The two habits the original hunt paid to learn

- **Cheap things do not wait.** If an action is an email, a page load,
  or a one-line question, it happens the day it is written down — or
  it is not written down at all. (Three of the original hunt's seven
  dead-in-queue leads were gated behind ONE unsent message that
  covered all of them.)
- **Do not "verify, then apply." Apply, and ask inside the
  application.** A verify step with no channel is not caution — it is
  a queue with an excuse attached. Often the application form IS the
  only contact channel.
